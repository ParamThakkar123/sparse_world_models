Abstract

Monolithic world models predict the entire next state at every step, spending capacity re-predicting the static majority and injecting error into it. We ask whether explicitly modeling what changes — a per-object change gate plus a residual delta head that perturbs only gated objects, copying the rest verbatim — is a more effective and interpretable bias.

On a MuJoCo tabletop pushing benchmark (3 to 8 objects), the sparse residual model is 2.5 to 4.6 times more accurate than a dense MLP at 8.6 to 11.1 times fewer parameters overall, but the durable win is error suppression on the static majority (F1 0.80 to 0.87, precision 0.92 to 0.97) with delta regression at roughly no-op quality, not accurate dynamics on movers. The gate transfers across counts (99.4 percent F1 retention), is more sample efficient, and when featurized for planner-visited states, sparse reaches 0.23 plus minus 0.06 success versus random 0.15 and dense 0.00 at every seed.

Why this page exists

The gate looks strong on overall L2, but most frames are trivially static. This demo scores only frames where something moved more than 2 cm and focuses on onset F1 — did you predict the hit on a box at rest? That is where nearest_to_pusher and already_moving expose the shortcut.

  • Sandbox — live physics, live scoring against 11 rules
  • 4 Engines — same shortcut on MuJoCo, Box2D, Chipmunk
  • More Boxes — same weights on 2 to 12 objects
  • Make It Plan — model as forward simulator for CEM

Method at a glance

Per object: shared gate network outputs a binary decision g, delta head outputs residual delta. Prediction is p_next = p + g * delta. Objects the gate leaves off are copied verbatim, so no injection. Train with class-balanced BCE on the gate, L2 on deltas only for changed objects, plus sparsity penalty.

Fig 1 in the paper shows the per-object head with weights shared across objects, independent of object count.

The playground idea

One finger, a few boxes kept apart. The closest box to the finger is almost always the one that moves. One number — distance — tells you the answer. Filters just pick which number.

What the AI is actually good at

Not detection — copying still boxes perfectly. Boxes the gate leaves OFF stay exactly put, so zero drift. Gap versus dense model grows with more boxes. Set boxes to 12 and watch.
